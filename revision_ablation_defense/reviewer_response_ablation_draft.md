## Reviewer 1 — R1.1 Component attribution

**Comment (provisional local wording; not authenticated):** “The main finding is driven primarily by B5, which simultaneously changes score blending, graph candidate expansion, ontology lookup, structured context, and other components. It is therefore difficult to determine which design choice causes the substantial quality degradation. A small controlled ablation of the main B5 components, particularly graph expansion, ontology enrichment, and structured context construction, would considerably strengthen the conclusions.”

**Response:** We agree that the original B1–B5 comparison changes multiple components together and cannot identify which component caused the historical B3–B5 quality difference. In response, we conducted an additional post-review sensitivity analysis using a freshly executed full B6 reference (paper-facing B5) and 13 component-off or interaction arms. The arms shared one frozen 375-query set (300 answerable, 75 synthetic designated-negative), one newly frozen `external_db` snapshot, the same generator/model revisions and tokenizer, and the CUDA/Torch runtime. This is a same-run, within-runtime component analysis; it is not a new B3 run, does not reproduce the historical July MLX environment, and does not decompose that historical B3–B5 contrast.

The matrix planned 5,250 query-arm operations. It produced 4,265 successful generations (each scored on all four rubric dimensions by the saved OmniRoute `codex/gpt-6-luna-low` judge) and 985 operations that failed before generation because the serialized prompt exceeded the configured 12,000-token context cap. Failed responses were retained as missing outcomes and were not scored zero. We report availability over all 375 queries separately from conditional judge quality among the same successful IDs in B6 and the compared arm.

The strongest matched-success result is the parent-chunk-deduplication intervention. Disabling deduplication changed Semantic Accuracy by +12.47 percentage points versus B6 (N=296, query-paired 95% bootstrap CI +7.88 to +16.97; exact paired sign-test p=2.09×10⁻⁶). This remains significant after Holm correction across the 13 post-hoc Semantic Accuracy contrasts (adjusted p=2.71×10⁻⁵) and across all 52 arm-by-four-metric contrasts (adjusted p=1.08×10⁻⁴). Faithfulness changed +10.20 pp (N=296, CI +6.05 to +14.33; 52-family adjusted p=.00548) and Answer Relevance +9.26 pp (CI +4.98 to +13.55; adjusted p=.0132); Citation Fidelity changed +9.36 pp, but did not remain significant after correction (52-family adjusted p=.481). The finding is conditional on jointly successful cases, one LLM judge, one frozen query set and one new database snapshot.

Disabling the dynamic GraphExpander is more mixed. On 296 jointly successful query IDs, Semantic Accuracy changed +7.44 pp (95% CI +2.22 to +12.77), but the sign-test result did not survive Holm correction (p=.361 within the Semantic Accuracy family; 1.0 across 52 contrasts). Faithfulness changed +7.33 pp (CI +2.29 to +12.11; 52-family adjusted p=.0220), whereas Citation Fidelity decreased −12.80 pp (CI −18.84 to −6.73; 52-family adjusted p=.00194). Answer Relevance changed +2.82 pp (CI −2.04 to +7.69). These effects do not support describing the expander as uniformly harmful or uniformly beneficial.

Answerability-stratified results underscore this trade-off. Among matched answerable queries, no-expander Semantic Accuracy changed +10.79 pp (N=248, 95% CI +5.24 to +16.27). Among matched designated-negative queries, the point estimate changed −9.90 pp (N=48, CI −25.00 to +4.58; interval crosses zero). The 75 negatives are synthetic designations and have not been independently human-validated as unanswerable from the entire corpus. Parser `UNKNOWN` is not abstention, and an unavailable generation is not an observed abstention or judged answer.

Saved context traces make evidence construction a plausible contributor, but not a proven mediator. With parent deduplication disabled, the five selected chunks averaged 1.97 unique PMIDs rather than 4.75 in B6, and the average number of chunks from the target/gold paper rose from .94 to 3.86. The observed chunk-level Context Precision increase (+55.96 pp) can count repeated chunks from the same gold PMID. A source-deduplicated document-level AP did not show more unique gold-source coverage in the paired cohort. We therefore do not conclude that repeated chunks caused the improved judged answers.

The ablation also identified non-informative downstream no-op arms: disabling static graph context and the fixed-budget context policy left B6-success prompts and outputs exactly identical on all 296 common cases, with exact ties on all judge metrics. These arms cannot establish a separate downstream answer-quality effect in this workload. Concept retrieval was disabled in its trace but altered only one of 296 prompt/response pairs; intrapaper-candidate disabling altered 24 of 294 common pairs. These results indicate little observed downstream activation in this cohort, not general ineffectiveness.

**Remaining limitation:** The additional run is a post-review, single-runtime sensitivity analysis, not a preregistered factorial study or historical B3 re-execution. It does not prove a unique cause for the July MLX B3–B5 difference. The reviewer concern is substantially answered with component-specific evidence, but causal attribution of the historical whole-system difference remains unresolved.
